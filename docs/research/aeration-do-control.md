# Aeration and dissolved-oxygen control

- **Checked:** 2026-09-15
- **Question:** What do owners, standards, and regulators actually declare for controlling diffused-air aeration — the control modes (constant-DO feedback versus airflow or header-pressure control, cascades, ammonia- or load-based feed-forward), the actuation forms and their combinations, multi-blower and multi-valve coordination against a shared air header, permissives and machine protections, DO-sensor failure behavior, energy and airflow accounting, the alarm set, and manual takeover — so that `WW-CTL-005` can carry observable acceptance criteria?
- **Affected requirements:** `WW-CTL-005`

## Supported findings

### Control modes owners specify

- Constant-DO feedback is the baseline mode everywhere: DO is measured by an on-line probe in the basin and the airflow to that zone is adjusted to hold the DO set-point [4 §6.3.2, 5 §4.3.1, 9, 13]. UFGS states it as an equipment obligation — control logic monitors DO signals and selects the number of blowers and the guide-vane setting or PD-blower speed to maintain the desired DO [6 §2.2.1.11.6]. Coweta has the PLC output a 4–20 mA airflow command per basin to maintain DO set-points, with DO and/or ORP probes controlling airflow to each grid [7 §3.4].
- The DO→airflow cascade is the standard arrangement, not an exotic one: a PI(D) controller computes the airflow change needed to restore the DO set-point and cascades a set-point to a separate airflow control loop [4 §6.3.3.1]; EPA 2010 describes the common cascaded form where the DO loop's output is the airflow controller's set-point, with a second loop between a header pressure transmitter and the blower [5 §4.3.1]. The two cascade families — airflow-based (each zone's valve throttles to a flow set-point) and pressure-based (a constant header pressure decouples the zones) — are documented as the industry's split, with pressure-based systems adding a supervisory set-point reset [18].
- Header-pressure control is an alternative primary mode: a PLC holds constant discharge header pressure while zones throttle [5 §4.3.1, 16]; under MOV variants the pressure set-point is not fixed but reset toward the minimum that still delivers demand — one valve held essentially full open [4 §6.3.3.1, 5 §4.3.1/Case Study 6, 18].
- Feed-forward exists as a specified layer: the 1989 manual's feedforward-feedback loop paces on an incoming disturbance (influent flow change) with the feedback loop trimming [4 §6.3.2.1]; AeroPAK offers a flow-paced mode using the DO loop output as a feedforward modifier of an influent-flow air demand [15]; SEDAC teaches model- or load-based feedforward paired with feedback correction [13]; the review cautions that ammonium feedforward adds sensors and complexity [9].
- Ammonia-based control is a specified supervisory variant: ABAC cascades an ammonia PID that resets the DO set-point from an effluent-side ISE probe [9, 13, 17]; AvN balances effluent ammonium against nitrate plus nitrite [14]; direct ammonium control replaces the DO sensor in the loop [13]. These presuppose the DO/airflow machinery below them — they change where the set-point comes from, not the actuation.
- Set-points are operator-visible and zoned: per-basin or per-grid DO set-points [4 §6.5.2–6.5.3, 7, 12], tapered profiles along a plug-flow basin [4 §6.3.3.1, 12], and the regulator-required residual capability — at least 1.0 mg/L unless process modeling justifies lower [3(e)], or the 0.2–0.5 mg/L SND band with >2.0 mg/L capability [8].
- Where the demand is calculated, the goal is matching a varying load: air volume varied in proportion to plant load demand [1 §92.332.e, 2]; the system designed to match diurnal organic load variation while economizing on power [3(h)]; DO adjustments to grids at 5–30-minute intervals with ±0.3–0.5 mg/L excursions tolerable [4 §5.4.3.1].

### Actuation forms and their combinations

- Blower capacity is modulated by speed or throttling depending on machine type: VFDs for positive-displacement blowers, inlet throttling or VFD for multistage centrifugal, variable speed plus inlet guide vanes and variable discharge diffuser vanes for single-stage centrifugal [5 §4.3.1]; inlet guide vanes are the specified element on single-stage machines [6 §2.2.1.1.11, 4 §6.5.1.3]. The 1989 design example controls IGVs against a motor-amperage set-point because power draw versus throughput is nearly linear at constant system pressure [4 §6.5.1.3].
- Per-zone air valves are the distribution actuators: motor-operated modulating butterfly valves per aeration grid with an electric actuator and a manual handwheel [7 §3.1]; dropleg valves, historically manual butterfly valves, retrofitted to modulating control [12, 5 Case Study 6]; valve flow characteristics (linear, equal-percentage, quick-opening) and actuator choice are engineering selections the control must live with [9].
- On/off blowers provide the capacity steps: bringing blowers online or offline changes the aeration rate, with a dead band around the DO set-point to prevent excessive starts/stops [4 §6.3.2.2]; blowers are started offline through a recycle/vent valve before joining the header [4 §6.5.1.2, 6].
- The typical combination is layered: DO loops move zone valves, a pressure loop (or MOV logic) sets blower capacity demand, and staging adds/removes machines at the extremes [4 §6.3.3.1, §6.5.1–6.5.2, 5 §4.3.1, 16]. Mechanical surface aerators — a different actuation form where the aerator is also the mixer — are within the same owner's aeration vocabulary [8 §3.4, 2(6)(b)] but pair with on/off or speed control and mixing constraints rather than valves.
- Instrumentation the actuation presumes: a mass airflow meter per controlled zone mounted downstream of its control valve, a pressure transmitter in the blower discharge header, and DO/ORP probes per grid [7 §3.4, 4 §6.5.2.3]; 4–20 mA is the command medium to valves and capacity elements [6, 7].

### Multi-blower and multi-valve coordination on the shared header

- Independent per-zone DO→valve loops fight through the shared header: raising airflow to one basin drops airflow and DO in another, and the controllers hunt; the documented remedy is a pressure control loop, with the header pressure set-point minimized so one valve always sits at its most-open position — the original MOV description [4 §6.3.3.1]. The same interaction is why the pressure-based and airflow-based cascade families exist [18].
- MOV is a distinct coordination layer, not part of the DO loop: keep the basin valves as open as possible — one valve at maximum position — and reset the header pressure (or the blower demand) to the minimum that still serves demand [4 §6.3.3.1, 5 §4.3.1, 16, 18]. Practice refines it to lowering the pressure set-point until the most-open valve sits in a declared band such as 85–95% open [19]. The alternative documented at Bucklin Point is direct flow control: MOV logic manipulating the valve positions themselves so one valve is always at maximum, eliminating the pressure loop entirely [5 Case Study 6].
- Blower staging is policy plus machinery constraints: the number of blowers online is selected to meet demand [6 §2.2.1.11.6]; a well-designed system uses each blower's full range to minimize starts and stops [4 §6.5.1.2]; lead/standby alternation is plant practice [16]; restrictions on minimum run time and time between starts must be respected [4 §6.5.1.2]. Bringing a unit online has documented choreography — start offline through a vent/recycle valve, sometimes reduce header pressure so its check valve can open, sometimes trim running blowers first to cap a demand-charge or power step [4 §6.5.1.2]; Madison's rotation procedure manually opens the distribution valves to drop header pressure, starts the second blower, then freezes the valves for ten minutes while the first shuts down to prevent a pressure-rise surge [4 §6.6.2].
- Coordination has hard bounds: the minimum-airflow set-point for mixing inside the control algorithm [4 §6.3.3.1]; Coweta's per-grid mixing timer with a computed cap on simultaneous mixing pulses and gradual airflow changes to avoid pressure swings at the blowers [7 §3.4]; surge and high-pressure limits on the machines (below); the N+1 sizing rule — maximum air demand with the largest unit out of service — plus turndown that avoids over-aeration at minimum flows without blowing off air [1, 2, 3].
- Whether blower staging is fully automatic is an owner choice: some control systems require operator approval before starting or non-emergency-stopping a blower; others only flag the need and the operation is manual [4 §6.5.1.2]; vendor packages offer automatic lead/lag [15]. Piscataway staged commissioning manual → day-shift → 24-hour automatic [4 §6.6.1].

### Permissives, interlocks, and machine protections

- Surge protection is mandatory for centrifugal machines and layered: manufacturers provide independent surge-protection systems and additional protection is incorporated in the control algorithm; interaction between the distribution and delivery controllers must be designed to keep line pressure down [4 §6.5.1.4]. UFGS requires a protective device that initiates the automatic shutdown sequence when the blower reaches surge volume — indicated by motor current draw — with visual indication of the shutdown reason and an override for startup and shutdown [6 §2.2.1.11.x]; a minimum-amperage limit is the common proxy for the surge region [4 §6.5.1.4], and Madison showed what happens when hardwired amp lockouts are not tied to the control system — the blowers surged before the operator could react [4 §6.6.2].
- Machine protection is a declared trip set, not advisory: blower protective devices on alarm de-energize the motor, run the automatic shutdown sequence, and raise audible and visual alarm — bearing over-temperature identifying the affected bearing, surge, vibration with alarm levels, motor overload controlled by throttling the inlet against a current set-point, and an automatic pressure relief valve on PD blowers [6 §2.2.1.11.x].
- Blower availability gates staging: N+1 units are a standards obligation [1 §92.332.e, 2, 3(a)] and the control logic selects among machines — which presumes an availability signal per blower [6 §2.2.1.11.6].
- The minimum-mixing floor is a permissive-like constraint on the demand path, not a process trip: the airflow set-point has a minimum to maintain mixing regardless of DO [4 §6.3.3.1]; Coweta's pulse scheme enforces it per grid [7 §3.4]; mixing requirements to keep solids suspended are a declared design factor [3(c)].
- High header pressure is both an efficiency bound (MOV exists to minimize it [16, 18]) and a protection boundary (high-pressure/surge interaction [4 §6.5.1.4, 6]).

### DO measurement and sensor-failure behavior

- Probe placement and count are engineered per basin: probes per basin near the center or the plug-flow inlet [5 §4.3.1]; one controlling probe per basin with others monitored by portable instrument in the low/moderate strategies, versus a probe per zone in the high-complexity strategy [4 §6.5.2]; multiple probes along a plug-flow basin to define the DO profile and locate the inflection point [4 §6.5.3].
- DO instrumentation is the acknowledged weak link: incorrectly applied or maintained instruments can render the control system useless — sensors are often considered the weakest link [4 §6.4.1]; Madison's probes needed cleaning every 3–4 days at high loading and calibration roughly every two weeks [4 §6.6.2]; self-cleaning probes and lower-maintenance optical sensors are the documented mitigations [5 §4.3.1.1].
- Redundant-measurement behavior is owner-specified in parts: the operator can select a specific bioreactor's grid probe or the average of the grid's probes across operating bioreactors to modulate blower output [12]; a backup controller function is specified for the analyzer terminal for reliability [7 §3.4]; multi-sensor voting or median-select logic is recommended practice for critical loops [19]. What the sources do **not** publish is a uniform fallback control mode when the controlling measurement is lost entirely — hold the last demand, revert to a fixed airflow, or drive to a safe maximum are the plausible candidates, and the choice is a declared-behavior question (see assumptions). The consistent floor is that a bad measurement must not silently control: sensor faults belong to the alarm set, and the manual/takeover path exists (below).
- Set-point and measurement drift is itself a documented failure mode — a probe reading artificially low forces the blowers to over-aerate and destroys the energy case [19]; on the air side, diffuser fouling raises the back-pressure the blower must push against, toward the surge region [4 §3.3, §6.5.1.4; 19].

### Energy and airflow accounting

- Aeration is the dominant energy load — 45–75% of facility electricity [10, 5 §4.1] — so measurement for energy purposes is specified, not optional: mass airflow meters per zone downstream of the control valves and a header pressure transmitter [7 §3.4]; a thermal mass flowmeter per blower discharge plus motor power signals logged at short intervals, with specific power (kW per 100 scfm) as the comparison metric [16].
- Savings are expressed in comparable metrics: kWh per kg of COD removed [18], percent of plant energy [5], 34% nitrification-aeration energy reduction at Piscataway [4 §6.6.1], and program guidance quoting 10–30% of total energy costs for automated DO control [13].
- The airflow signal doubles as a process diagnostic: with DO controlled, oxygen uptake rate can be estimated from airflow, and changes in the αF(KLa)-versus-airflow relationship flag diffuser fouling and maintenance needs [4 §6.3.3.1–6.3.3.2].
- Whether per-zone and per-train airflow must be *totalized* and reported (as opposed to measured for control) is thinner in the sources — the meters above are specified for control and energy evaluation [3(a), 7 §3.4, 16], but no aeration-specific totalization or reporting mandate was found; record as an open assumption tied to `WW-REP-001`.

### The alarm set an aeration train raises

- Equipment protection alarms compose from the per-blower set: bearing over-temperature (identifying which bearing), surge shutdown with reason indication, vibration level, motor overload/fault, oil-system status, and the startup/shutdown override state — UFGS requires audible and visual alarm on protective-device action plus the visual reason indication [6 §2.2.1.11.x]; the per-equipment-class minimum alarm tables include blowers [11].
- Process alarms: DO outside its band per zone (low-DO being the permit-relevant direction [3(e), 19]), airflow below the mixing floor [4 §6.3.3.1, 7 §3.4], header pressure outside bounds, and the instrument-fault family — probe fault/discrepancy, airflow-meter fault, pressure-transmitter fault [4 §6.4.1, 7 §3.4, 19].
- Control-health indications: backup/manual mode engagement, MOV or pressure set-point at its bound (the coordinator can no longer optimize), blower staging requests pending operator approval where that option is configured [4 §6.5.1.2], and communication/PLC faults reported separately from process alarms.
- Owners require the alarm list as a deliverable — an alarm and I/O list is a specified submittal [7 §1.3] — i.e., the set is declared per plant, not emergent.

### Manual and local takeover

- Manual override of the automation is assumed at every layer: valves carry a handwheel for manual operation [7 §3.1]; blower start/stop can require an operator-approval step or run flag-only/manual [4 §6.5.1.2]; Madison's operators manually override blower control and throttle guide vanes to stage a rotation [4 §6.6.2]; low-complexity strategies keep distribution valves manually adjusted by design, with the operator keeping them collectively as open as possible [4 §6.5.2.1–6.5.2.2].
- Commissioning evidence favors staged adoption — manual control with monitoring, then supervised automatic on day shift, then unattended automatic [4 §6.6.1] — so the operator surface must let each layer run independently before the next is enabled.
- A local control panel with PLC/HMI is the packaged-equipment pattern [7 §3.3]; what the operator should be able to hold in remote-manual (per-zone airflow demand? blower demand? both?) is a customer question, but the safe-state on manual is the same shape as the station's — declared protections (surge, min mixing airflow, machine trips) persist while the operator holds the demand.

## Existing seams versus contract gaps

What the current component kinds, descriptors, and typed-build seam already express:

- Per-zone DO feedback: `analog-input` + `signal-filter` + `pid` express the DO loop [4 §6.3.2, 5 §4.3.1]; the set-point is a writable internal `In` point or a `set_parameter` target; `pid.out_min/out_max` bound the demand.
- Cascade wiring: a `pid`'s `out` wired through a port→port internal link onto another `pid`'s `sp` expresses DO→airflow and ammonia→DO-set-point cascades [4 §6.3.3.1, 9, 17] — at the cost of one scan of delay per cascade level (decision 4's link semantics).
- Valve and machine actuation: `valve` covers a modulating air valve with position feedback and a discrepancy flag [7 §3.1]; `motor` covers a blower's run command with failed-feedback diagnostics; `analog-output` carries a speed or guide-vane demand [5 §4.3.1, 6].
- DO measurement redundancy: `median-voter` votes three probes with a spread `discrepancy` flag and worst-quality propagation — the voting form [19] recommends; `failover-select` covers the primary/backup-probe and probe-select forms [7, 12].
- Permissives and trips: `interlock` (permissives plus trips to a safe value), `bool-gate`, `sr-latch`, and debounced `digital-input` cover blower-availability gating, machine protective trips, and high-pressure inhibits [6 §2.2.1.11.x]; `alarm-monitor`/`threshold-chain` express the pressure/flow/DO threshold flags.
- Group staging precedent: `pump-group`'s indexed `cmd_i`/`run_i`/`fault_i`/`avail_i` family, declared rotation policy, and checkpointed group state [1, 2, 4 §6.5.1.2] demonstrate the per-member pattern — for on/off members only.
- Demand shaping and manual takeover: `rate-limiter` covers the gradual airflow changes and ramped moves [7 §3.4]; `manual-station` gives bumpless manual takeover of an analog demand; `override-select` chooses demand sources; `timer`/`counter` express a per-grid mixing timer [7 §3.4].
- Accounting and alarms: `totalizer` integrates an airflow rate; `latching-alarm`/`bool-latching-alarm`/`alarm-monitor` compose the equipment and process alarm set [6, 7, 11].

What they cannot express — the gap list WW-CTL-005 implementation tickets would consume:

1. **Shared-header coordination — the central question.** Nothing selects the most-open of N valve positions, resets a header-pressure set-point so that valve sits in a declared band, or splits a total air demand across running blowers [4 §6.3.3.1, 5 §4.3.1/Case 6, 16, 18, 19]. It is a many-input supervisory function over the zone valves plus the header pressure; `pump-group` stages on/off members rather than optimizing a shared continuous resource, and no kind computes a max-of-N selection a wiring convention could fake. Whether it lands as a new header-coordinator kind (`valve_pos_i`/`pressure`/`airflow_i` in, `pressure_sp`/`blower_demand` out, declared strategy = constant-pressure | MOV | direct-airflow), a documented wiring convention, or `dcs-build` builder-level composition is the recorded decision the first implementation ticket must take — the same shape as the filter-bank arbiter question in `docs/research/filter-backwash.md`.
2. **Blower-group semantics beyond `pump-group`.** Blower staging needs continuous capacity: a demand-split or capacity-set-point per running machine, per-unit min/max flow and current bounds, stage-up/stage-down thresholds, minimum run time and time between starts [4 §6.5.1.2], the start-offline-through-vent choreography [4 §6.5.1.2, 6], and the valve-freeze/pressure-relief transition during rotation [4 §6.6.2]. `pump-group` covers the on/off staging skeleton but none of the continuous or sequenced parts. → a `blower-group` kind, or a declared extension of the pump-group pattern plus a `sequencer` start/stop routine.
3. **Surge and machine-guard contract.** The surge region is a flow-versus-pressure boundary guard on the demand path [4 §6.5.1.4, 6 §2.2.1.11.x]; `interlock` trips a Bool to a safe value, but "clamp demand above the declared minimum-flow/minimum-current bound and below the pressure bound, and trip on a proven surge" is a two-variable guard with a declared response. → declared bounds plus a surge-trip input on the blower contract, or a small guard kind; the machine's own protective devices remain hardwired and only report status [6].
4. **Declared DO-loss fallback.** `median-voter` and `failover-select` produce a voted/selected measurement, but when all DO sources are non-`Good` the loop needs a declared `on_bad` response — hold last demand, revert to a fixed airflow, or drive to a declared safe airflow — alarmed on transition. This is the `threshold-chain.on_bad_demand` pattern applied to the DO→airflow demand path; the sources evidence the measurement-redundancy forms but leave the fallback mode to be declared [12, 19; absence noted above].
5. **Feed-forward demand injection.** Flow-paced or load-proportional air demand with feedback trim [4 §6.3.2.1, 13, 15] is the same ratio/feed-forward gap WW-CTL-003 records for dosing — a computed demand summed with a feedback trim is not expressible by composing `pid`s. Whether the dosing ratio kind and the aeration feed-forward share one contract is a design decision for the implementing tickets.
6. **Per-grid mixing-pulse coordination.** Coweta's pulse scheme needs a plant-wide cap on simultaneously pulsing grids — a cross-instance constraint no per-grid timer can see [7 §3.4]. → a small coordinator output or a declared interlock composition; a candidate to fold into the header-coordinator decision (gap 1).
7. **Blower device contract.** Whether a blower is a device kind wrapping `motor` + `analog-output` speed/vane demand + the protective-status family, or stays a `motor` + `analog-output` + `interlock` composition, is a `dcs-build` assembly decision — the same metering-pump question the dosing note records.
8. **Energy/specific-power accounting.** `totalizer` covers airflow volume; specific power (kW per 100 scfm) and kWh-per-load metrics [16, 18] need a computed ratio — there is no arithmetic derived-value kind — or live in reporting (`WW-REP-001`). Recorded as an open seam, not a blocker.
9. **Mechanical-aerator scope.** Surface/jet aerators [8 §3.4, 2(6)(b)] are on/off-or-speed machines with a mixing constraint — closer to `motor` composition than to the diffused-air train. Whether WW-CTL-005's contract covers them or they remain plain `motor` instances is a scoping decision for the planner.

## Proposed DCS implications

*All items in this section are proposals, not decided behavior.*

- Model each controlled aeration zone as a declared composition: a DO `pid` (or the zone's declared controller) resetting an airflow demand, bounded by a declared minimum-mixing airflow floor and maximum airflow, driving a `valve` (or an airflow `pid` driving the `valve` in the full cascade) [4 §6.3.3.1, §6.5.2, 7 §3.4]. Declare `on_bad` DO behavior on the demand path with an alarmed transition.
- Model the shared header as a declared coordination strategy with three evidenced forms — constant-pressure control, MOV pressure reset, and direct-airflow control — rather than baking one in [4 §6.3.3.1, 5 §4.3.1/Case 6, 16, 18]: whatever the contract shape (new kind vs. convention vs. builder composition, gap 1), the strategy choice and its bounds (pressure limits, most-open-valve band, minimum total airflow) are declared parameters.
- Model blower capacity as a group concern: staging on demand with declared min-run-time and start-interval bounds, declared rotation, per-unit availability and fault exclusion, and the documented start/stop choreography (offline start via vent, header-pressure relief to join, delivery trim before adding a unit) carried as a declared sequence or as declared bounds on the group contract [4 §6.5.1.2, §6.6.2, 6].
- Carry machine protection as declared trips that outrank every demand: surge (minimum-flow/minimum-current guard plus a proven-surge trip input), bearing temperature, vibration, motor overload, high header pressure — the protective-device set [6 §2.2.1.11.x] — each driving the machine to its safe state and raising the alarm with reason indication.
- Support the supervisory variants as set-point wiring, not new machinery: ABAC is an ammonia `pid` whose `out` is the DO loop's `sp`; feedforward is a computed demand summed with the feedback trim — both on declared `on_bad` behavior of their own [9, 13, 17].
- Compose the alarm set from `latching-alarm`/`bool-latching-alarm`/`alarm-monitor` instances fed by the coordinator, group, and zone status outputs: per-zone low/high DO, airflow below the mixing floor, header pressure bounds, per-blower protection trips with reason, staging-pending-approval, measurement discrepancy/fault, and the fallback-engaged transition [4, 6, 7, 11].
- Keep manual/local takeover as `manual-station` on each demand plus the equipment's local/remote indication and the valve's manual facility [4 §6.5.1.2, §6.5.2, 7 §3.1]; declare which protections persist in manual (the machine trips and the mixing floor — matching the station pattern where protections outrank the operator's demand).
- Carry zone set-points, coordinator state (most-open-valve identity, pressure set-point), and blower-group state (staging position, accumulated run hours) through checkpoints so a controller promotion does not step the air demand — the aeration-facing reach of `WW-LCM-001` (a proposal; sources do not address controller redundancy).

## Assumptions needing customer validation

- Which control mode is primary for the first client: per-zone constant-DO feedback, the DO→airflow cascade, or header-pressure control as the outer loop — and whether an ammonia-based supervisory layer (ABAC/AvN) or load feed-forward is in scope for the first deployment [4, 5, 9, 13, 14, 17].
- The header-coordination strategy and its declared bounds: constant header pressure, MOV pressure reset (and the most-open-valve band, e.g. 85–95% [19]), or direct-airflow control without a pressure loop [5 Case Study 6] — and whether it lands as a new kind, a wiring convention, or builder composition (gap 1).
- The declared response when every DO measurement for a zone is non-`Good`: hold last airflow demand, revert to a declared fixed airflow, or a declared safe maximum — and whether it is operator-selectable; the sources evidence probe selection/averaging/voting [12, 19] but not the terminal fallback mode.
- The DO measurement architecture per zone: single probe, primary-plus-backup, averaging [12], or three-probe voting [19] — and whether discrepancy flags alarm, latch, or merely flag.
- Blower staging policy: fully automatic stage-up/down, operator-approval required, or flag-only/manual [4 §6.5.1.2]; the rotation policy among machines; and the per-unit bounds (min run time, starts per interval, min/max flow and current) that are machine data rather than library defaults [4 §6.5.1.2, 6].
- Which actuation forms the client's blowers present — speed, IGV/inlet throttling, discharge diffuser vanes, on/off — and whether a single `blower` device contract or a `motor` + `analog-output` composition represents them [5 §4.3.1, 6].
- The required alarm set and latch policy per plant [7 §1.3, 11], including whether the fallback-engaged and staging-pending states alarm.
- Whether airflow totalization and specific-energy (kW/scfm, kWh per load) reporting live in the control contract or in `WW-REP-001` reporting [16, 18].
- Whether mechanical surface/jet aerators are inside WW-CTL-005's scope or remain plain `motor` compositions [8 §3.4].
- Whether the mixing-pulse coordination (per-grid timer plus a plant-wide simultaneous-pulse cap [7 §3.4]) is required for the first client or specific to pulsed-aeration designs.

**Revisit condition:** WW-CTL-005 stays implementable as declared-policy composition; promote the *coordination contract* question (gap 1) to a design decision before any implementation ticket, and revisit this requirement if a first-client specification fixes the header strategy, the DO-loss fallback, or the staging authority in a way the declared-parameter framing cannot express.
