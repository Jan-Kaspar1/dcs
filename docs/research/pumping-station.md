## Supported findings

### Operating modes and equipment behavior

- Duplex duty/standby is the baseline municipal station: multiple pumps are required; where only two are provided they are the same size, and the remaining units must carry the design peak hourly flow with any unit out of service [1 §42.31]. Stations up to roughly 700 gpm customarily install two pumps each able to meet the maximum influent rate [2]. Larger stations add lag pumps under a lead/lag/standby naming — one lead plus standby up to a threshold flow, then one or more lags [12].
- Automatic alternation is standard owner/regulator language: "provision shall be made to automatically alternate the pumps in use" [1 §42.4]; pumps "alternate as lead and lag on a continuous basis" [9]; alternation occurs after each pump-down cycle [6]; all pumps including standby must alternate [12]. The *policy* varies by owner: per-cycle alternation [6, 9], daily rather than per-cycle alternation for suction-lift stations to protect priming equipment [1 §42.4], an explicit prohibition on alternating at every start [4], and accumulated-run-time balancing (stage/destage by operating hours, FIFO/LIFO orders) in multi-pump controllers [18, 19]. Hours-run metering per pump is standard equipment [9, 11].
- Demand assist is level-driven: the lag pump starts when the level reaches the lag-start setpoint because the lead pump cannot hold the inflow — or because the lead pump failed [4, 9]; on falling level the lag stops first at its own stop point [4]. A time delay prevents both pumps starting simultaneously [9, 10].
- Cycling limits are engineered: wet-well active volume and setpoints are sized against maximum starts per hour — six per hour [7], or eight normal / ten worst-case [8] — and stations carry maximum run-time criteria [8]. A de-staged pump may be held unavailable for a restage delay [19].
- Equipment permissives, unavailability, and mode selection are first-class: ready/available inputs gate which pumps may stage [19]; maintenance, hand, and override modes exist beside operator and program control [18]; H-O-A switches per pump are required hardware [9, 11]; motor over-temperature and moisture sensors lock the pump out [10].
- Response to a running pump's failed feedback: run-feedback disagreement within a configured time raises fail-to-start or fail-to-stop [18]; a duty pump that fails to start or is unavailable hands duty to the standby, which is called to start instead [5]; a lead pump failure calls the lag [4]; failure-to-pump is detectable from check-valve limit switches and shuts the pump down to prevent damage [11]; a fail-to-start may latch a shed state requiring reset before restart [18].

### Station control semantics

- Threshold control is the dominant semantics: an ordered setpoint chain — low-water cut-off, lead start, lead stop, lag start, high-level alarm — drives pump calls [4, 10]; setpoints are operator-adjustable through the HMI [5] and are submitted for owner review during design [7].
- Continuous level control is the variable-speed alternative: variable-speed pumping holds the wet well near constant elevation, matches discharge to inflow, and minimizes starts and stops [2]; owner criteria still bound it (minimum-speed cycling when inflow falls below the minimum pumping rate) [8]. WEF MOP FD-4's instrumentation and control chapter covers both practices for the station class [3].
- Bad-measurement behavior is engineered, not improvised: an independent hardwired float-backup mode runs pumps from dedicated high-level floats when the transmitter chain fails, electrically separate from the controller [6]; transducer failure falls back to a four-point float scheme [10]; dedicated controllers carry a backup-float input plus a recommended separate alarm float [20]; multiple transmitters per wet well feed a "master level" selection that fails over on duty-transmitter loss and alarms on inter-transmitter deviation [5]; bubbler level systems require dual air compressors [1 §42.4].
- Manual takeover is a distinct mode: HAND operation is a separate circuit per pump [6]; in HAND the low-level cut-off may not protect the pump while motor over-temperature still does [6]; backup-mode engagement and bypass are themselves alarmed and indicated per pump [5].

### Alarm expectations

- The minimum station alarm set is owner/regulator-defined: power failure, pump failure, dry-well sump and wet-well high water, unauthorized entry, or any other station malfunction, on a backed-up supply [1 §46]. Owner lists add per-pump failure-to-start, failure-to-pump (check-valve), thermal and moisture faults, float/transducer failure, VFD failure, and generator running/failed [10, 11]; backup-control-mode engagement is alarmed as critical [5].
- Alarm lifecycle follows the standard state model: normal, unacknowledged, acknowledged, cleared-unacknowledged (return-to-normal before ack), plus shelved, suppressed-by-design, and out-of-service as managed states [13 §5.3]; the alarm system includes an alarm and event log, an alarm historian, and performance metrics [14]; prioritisation and flood-handling guidance sits in [15].
- Operator visibility assumes unattended stations: alarms with condition identification are transmitted to a facility staffed 24 hours, else to municipal offices and responsible personnel [1 §46]; local audio-visual alarms supplement telemetry [1, 9]; SCADA presents alarms with criticality marking [5]. Some owner panels require a latched alarm reset by the operator (e.g., the high-level-alarm reset in [10]'s fault list).

### Commissioning workflow

- The engineered artifacts an engineer produces and the owner reviews: wet-well sizing and control elevations with emergency storage, proposed start/stop levels for lead and lag pumps, high-level alarm setpoints, and run-time calculations against starts-per-hour limits [7]; a written process control narrative of the intended behavior [5 is itself an owner-standard PCN]; the alarm set per the owner list [1 §46, 10, 11].
- On-site verification: documented startup and testing supervised by the manufacturer's representative with the owner present and written results [7 §5.4]; continuous full-load pump runs, vibration checks, and system testing of all automated equipment before final inspection [16 §13.1.07]; SCADA point testing, controller FAT, performance testing including SAT, and a defect checklist before handover [17]. Alarm work follows the lifecycle's implementation stage — alarms configured, tested, and trained before operation [13 §5.2].

## Existing seams versus contract gaps

What the current component kinds, descriptors, and typed-build seam already express:

- Per-equipment actuation with failed-feedback diagnostics: `motor` echoes the command and flags `fault` after `fault_ticks` consecutive disagreeing scans — covering fail-to-start and fail-to-stop detection [18]; `valve` does the same for analog position with a tolerance. Quality merging already marks an untrusted feedback.
- Permissive and trip gating: `interlock` (permissive plus trip inputs driving a safe value) and `bool-gate`/`sr-latch` cover interlock chains; `digital-input` debounce conditions float inputs.
- Alarm lifecycle: `alarm-monitor` (auto-clearing limit with hysteresis) and `latching-alarm` (two-flag `alarm`/`unacknowledged` with a writable `ack` point, journaled acknowledgment, checkpointed latch) match the acknowledged/unacknowledged axis of [13 §5.3]; shelving, suppression, and classes are the recorded decision-38 deferral, not missing machinery.
- Manual takeover on an analog demand: `manual-station` gives a bumpless (slewed) operator/manual transfer; `override-select` selects sources; writable internal `In` points carry operator setpoints and mode switches through the receipted command path; `set_parameter` tunes declared parameters with descriptor-declared ranges — the mechanism for operator-adjustable start/stop setpoints [5].
- Continuous level control and measurement redundancy: `pid`, `analog-input`, `signal-filter`, and `median-voter` (2oo3 voting with spread discrepancy — the nearest existing semantic to [5]'s master-level selection).
- Timing and accounting: `timer`, `counter` (start counting for starts-per-hour checks), `totalizer` (integrates a Float rate — flow totals), `sequencer` (fixed step tables).
- Presentation and composition: per-kind `ComponentDescriptor`s feeding descriptor-driven faceplates, `Signal.group` and the signal index, bounded history and the journal (persisted by the journal file), the pair-as-one-controller view, and the `dcs-build` typed-build path emitting the versioned model — the composition seam the reference station is built through.

What they cannot express — the gap list M9's library tickets implement:

1. **Duty/standby group coordination.** No kind decides which of N pumps is duty, rotates that assignment by a declared policy (each-cycle alternation [6, 9], timed/daily [1], accumulated run hours [19]), stages a lag pump on unmet demand [4], excludes unavailable pumps [5, 19], or reassigns duty when a running pump's feedback fails [5, 18]. Composing this from gates and latches would re-implement the policy per station with no named semantics. → a pump-group (duty/standby manager) component kind.
2. **Per-pump availability aggregation.** "Available" — not faulted, not locally selected, not maintenance-inhibited, permissives satisfied — is spec vocabulary [11, 18] that today would be ad-hoc `bool-gate` wiring with no standard descriptor meaning. → per-pump availability inputs on the group kind, or a small availability kind.
3. **Run-hours accumulation and least-run selection.** `totalizer` integrates a Float rate; accumulating Bool run-feedback hours and ranking pumps by them [19] has no feed. → group-internal accumulators or a runtime-accumulator kind.
4. **Start/stop threshold control.** The ordered setpoint chain (low cut-off, duty start/stop, lag start, high alarm [4, 10]) can be hand-wired from `alarm-monitor` plus `sr-latch`, but as station semantics it wants one declared setpoint table with hysteresis and quality behavior. → a threshold-controller kind or group-level parameters.
5. **Degraded-measurement failover mode.** Float backup [6, 10, 20] and master-level failover [5] are failure-*driven* source switches with their own alarmed mode state; `override-select` switches on an operator input, not on quality, and `median-voter` is 2oo3 voting, not primary/backup failover. → a quality-driven selector/failover kind, or the station assembly's declared backup mode.
6. **Group-raised station alarms.** "No pump available", "all pumps faulted", "station in backup control" need the group's status outputs; they then compose into `latching-alarm` instances with no further contract work [1 §46, 5]. No new alarm contract — but the group must expose the conditions.
7. **Station assembly composition.** A duty/standby station as one typed artifact — N motors, level measurement, threshold or continuous control, the alarm set, operator points — is a `dcs-build` composition concern; whether it lands as a component kind, a builder-level assembly spec, or both is an open architecture decision for the planner.

## Proposed DCS implications

*All items in this section are proposals, not decided behavior.*

- Model the reference station as a duplex (or lead/lag/standby-capable) pump group where duty selection, rotation, lag assist, and fault-driven reassignment are one declared contract — matching the group semantics every owner spec above assumes.
- Make the rotation policy a declared choice — alternate each cycle, alternate on a timed interval, or least-run-hours first — because owner policies demonstrably differ [1 §42.4 vs 4 vs 6 vs 19]; ship a per-cycle default for the reference station.
- Treat per-pump availability as an explicit computed state (fault, local/manual selection, maintenance inhibit, permissives) that the group consumes, so an unavailable duty hands over per [5].
- Support both station control semantics: a declared threshold setpoint table as the default [4, 7, 9, 10], and a continuous `pid` level mode for variable-speed stations [2, 8]; on bad measurement, fail over to a declared backup source (float-backup mode [6, 10, 20] or master-level selection [5]) and alarm the transition.
- Compose the station alarm set — high level, low level/dry-run, per-pump fail-to-start/fail-to-stop, measurement fault, backup-mode active, station power/communication fault — from `latching-alarm` instances so acknowledgment follows [13]'s states; keep shelving, suppression, and priority classes deferred per decision 38.
- Treat commissioning as: engineer declares the model and setpoint table through `dcs-build`; `dcs-model` validate/lint and `dcs-controller --check` stand in for the design-review submittal [7]; simulation stands in for the witnessed startup tests [7 §5.4, 16, 17].
- Carry group run state — rotation position, accumulated hours, unacknowledged latches, tuned setpoints — through checkpoints so promotion and restart preserve the duty assignment and operator-visible state (the station-facing reach of `WW-LCM-001`; no source addresses controller redundancy directly — see assumptions).

## Assumptions needing customer validation

- The representative station is a constant-speed duplex lift station with duty/standby; whether the first client runs VFD constant-level control as primary is unconfirmed — WW-CTL-002 keeps both until validated.
- Which rotation policy the client specifies: per-cycle, timed, or hours-run; and whether standby pumps participate in alternation [12 says yes; 4 says no per-start alternation].
- Whether a failed duty pump hands over automatically to standby [5, 4] or alarms and waits; and whether the failed unit is latched out until operator reset [18].
- The measurement architecture: single transmitter plus hardwired float backup [6, 10, 20], or redundant transmitters with master selection [5]; and the declared behavior when *all* level measurement is bad (hold last duty, stop, or run on backup floats only).
- The required alarm set, which alarms latch until acknowledged, any priority classes, and the telemetry destination [1 §46 expects a 24-hour staffed facility].
- Manual-takeover scope: panel-only HAND, remote manual over SCADA, or both; and which protections persist in manual (Silver Lake drops the low-level cut-off but keeps over-temperature [6]).
- Which commissioning artifacts the client requires: process control narrative, setpoint submittal, witnessed point tests, FAT/SAT records [5, 7, 16, 17].
- Controller redundancy is this product's differentiator, not owner-specified: sources treat stations as unattended with telemetry alarms [1 §46]; that duty rotation state and unacknowledged alarms must survive takeover is our proposal, not evidence.
