# Operating reports, regulatory records, energy metrics, and maintenance exports

- **Checked:** 2026-09-15
- **Question:** What do regulators, owner specifications, and sector guidance actually require a water or wastewater control system to *record or feed* — the periodic operating reports (state monthly operating reports, NPDES discharge monitoring reports) and their reporting cadence; the continuously recorded turbidity and disinfection records behind them; alarm, event, and operator-action record retention; the maintenance data owners specify (run-hours, starts-per-period, equipment-fault histories); the energy and specific-energy metrics utilities track; and in what form owners specify each — historian extracts, periodic report artifacts, or merely observable telemetry — so that `WW-REP-001` can carry either observable acceptance criteria or a concrete revisit condition?
- **Affected requirements:** `WW-REP-001`; edges touch `WW-CTL-004` (the per-filter turbidity record the filter-bank events feed — `docs/research/filter-backwash.md`), `WW-CTL-005` (energy/specific-power accounting — `docs/research/aeration-do-control.md` gap 8), `WW-OPS-002` and `WW-ALM-001`–`004` (the alarm/event record — `docs/research/alarm-management.md`), and `WW-LCM-002`/`WW-SEC-001` (journal retention, export, and integrity — `docs/research/deployment-commissioning.md` gap 6, `docs/research/security-identity.md`).

## Supported findings

### Periodic operating reports are certified artifacts built over recorded periods

- Drinking-water systems file a monthly report with the State within 10 days after each month [4, 5]; NPDES permittees file DMRs at the intervals the permit specifies [1(l)(4), 2], plus biosolids, pretreatment, and sewer-overflow/bypass reports [3]. The contents are period aggregates: counts and percentages of turbidity measurements within limits, daily minimum residuals, excursion dates and durations, quarterly and running annual averages [4, 8] — computation over the period's recorded data, not point values.
- Submission is electronic and certified: part 127 routes DMRs through NetDMR or state eDMR systems with part-3 signature and certification standards [3]. The report artifact, its format, and its certification are the permittee's obligations — sources place them in a reporting layer fed by plant data, not in the control system that produces the measurements [5, 15, 21].
- Event-driven reporting sits beside the periodic artifact: 24-hour consultation on turbidity above 5 NTU, next-business-day notification on residual below 0.2 mg/L plus whether it was restored within 4 hours, 24-hour oral and 5-day written reporting of endangering noncompliance [4, 1(l)(6)–(7)]. The control system's contribution is the alarmed detection and the timestamped record the operator reports from.

### Continuously recorded process records are the regulatory feedstock

- Individual-filter effluent turbidity is a continuously monitored record: measurement per filter, results recorded at least every 15 minutes, results retained at least 3 years, and monthly confirmation that the monitoring was conducted [6; 9]. Exceedances are defined over the *recorded* series — two consecutive 15-minute recordings above 1.0 NTU — so the record's cadence and continuity are themselves compliance-relevant, and a monitoring gap is a reportable failure (the turbidity guidance manual's 14-day monitoring-failure limit [6]).
- The daily disinfection record is similarly structured: daily lowest residual, contact time, pH, temperature, daily CT values and the summed inactivation determination [4(a)(2)] — a per-day series the monthly report reduces.
- Backwash operations carry their own retention list where recycle is practiced: recycle flows and their return frequency, average and maximum backwash flow rates and durations, typical filter run length and how it is determined [7] — the per-backwash event record the filter bank produces (firing trigger, step timing, volumes) is feedstock, matching Water Corporation's requirement that the firing trigger be recorded per event [filter-backwash note, its source 8].
- Calibration and maintenance records are expressly part of the retained monitoring record [1(j)(2)] — the regulatory record boundary includes instrument upkeep, not only process values.

### Alarm, event, and operator-action records

- IEC 62682's alarm-system scope includes the alarm and event log, the alarm historian, and performance metrics [alarm-management note, its source 2]; monitoring-and-assessment operates over that record, and water-sector practice gathers alarm statistics continuously for periodic review [its sources 6, 13]. Owner standards require an Operator Event Log [19] and evaluate flow totals and runtimes [15 §3.15].
- Retention expectations split by record type: the multi-year regulatory periods bind monitoring and compliance records [1(j)(2), 6, 8]; the operator-event and alarm record's retention is owner policy and alarm-management practice rather than a fixed federal term — the same conclusion the lifecycle and security notes reach for the journal [deployment-commissioning gap 6; security-identity note].

### Maintenance data owners specify at the control layer

- Per-motor run-time accumulators are specified outright: total run hours plus current-day and yesterday's run hours per motor [17]; per-pump previous-day and current-day run hours and a pump-called count as named SCADA signals [16]; starts and run-time counters plus an event history on packaged pump panels [22]. Starts-per-period matters because starts-per-hour is an engineered sizing and control bound (pumping-station note: six starts per hour; `pump-group`'s `min_off_ticks`).
- Equipment-fault and event histories are the same pattern: the packaged panel's 4000-event timestamped log [22] and the owner Operator Event Log requirement [19] describe a durable, per-equipment event record.
- The export direction is SCADA→CMMS: runtime hours and alarm events mapped per-asset into a maintenance system that turns them into preventive-maintenance triggers and work orders [23] — the control system supplies the counters and events; the maintenance artifact lives downstream.

### Energy metrics are period computations over metered quantities

- The specified measurements are energy and flow: station and per-pump power monitors reporting kW/kWh/total-kWh [16]; blower-discharge airflow meters plus motor power with specific power (kW per 100 scfm) logged at short intervals as the comparison metric [14]; energy intensity in kWh/MG as the facility metric [11, 12, 13] and kWh per unit load removed for aeration processes [14].
- Every metric is a ratio or a period total computed downstream of the meters: kWh/MG is a billing-period or period energy total over a flow total [10, 13]; kW/100 scfm is an instantaneous-or-logged ratio [14]; running annual averages are report-layer math [8]. No source found requires the *control system* to compute these — the obligation is that the measured quantities exist, are totalized, and are retrievable.
- Energy management guidance assumes interval data exists to analyze: monthly utility data at minimum, daily or hourly intervals preferred [10] — i.e., a history of the metered quantities, which is the historian question again.

### The form owners specify: telemetry, historian extract, or report artifact

- *Observable telemetry*: tag-list conventions define per-point signals including PLC-computed internals (run-hours, pump-called counts, backup-mode states) [16, 17] — present-tense values and counters on the monitoring surface.
- *Historian/extract*: all-I/O historization pushed to a shared database for other consumers [15], a standby doubling as the historic database [18] — the long-term store is a deployment role; the controller feeds it.
- *Report artifacts*: monthly/quarterly regulator forms and daily operator reports, produced by a report generator or data-management product from the historized record, on a schedule or trigger [5, 15, 21]. The certified-submission path (NetDMR, state forms, part-3 signatures) is unambiguously downstream of the control system [3].

## What the implemented surfaces already feed versus contract gaps

What the current kinds, record, and monitoring surface already supply:

- **Period totals and counters.** `totalizer` integrates a Float `rate` with `reset`, `rollover`, and frozen-on-bad-quality semantics, checkpointed (#111); `counter` accumulates Bool rising edges with `preset`/`done`/`reset` (#85). These carry flow totals, backwash volumes [20], pump-called and starts counts [16, 17, 22], and — fed a power signal — energy totals.
- **Checkpointed run state.** `pump-group` accumulates per-pump `run_hours_i` (in ticks) inside its checkpointed state map, so run-hours survive restart and promotion — the duty-rotation record and the raw material of the owners' run-hours signals [16, 17].
- **Volatile per-point history.** The decision-17 history ring records every point's every-scan sample bounded by `MonitorConfig`, served at `GET /history` and drawn by the page's trend panes [15 trending] — short-horizon process history with quality and tick stamps; bounded eviction is visible as `seq` gaps, not silent loss.
- **The durable attributed journal.** `--journal-file` appends every journal entry — actor-attributed `CommandSettled` receipts, `QualityChanged` transitions, `StepFailed` component errors, `RoleChanged`, `DivergenceDetected`, `Reinitialized` — across restarts with run-boundary markers (decisions 17, 36, 39; #163, #165): the operator-action and event record the Operator-Event-Log and audit expectations describe [19; alarm note]. `GET /journal` is its served surface; `dcs-ctl` reads it.
- **Observable telemetry and alarming.** The page's trend and alarm surfaces plus the two-flag alarm lifecycle (`alarm` standing state, `unacknowledged` latch, journaled writable-point `ack`) expose present values, standing alarms, and acknowledgment — the "what is happening now" layer [15 §3.10 trending; 16].

What would need contract work or a recorded decision — the gap list WW-REP-001 implementation tickets would consume:

1. **Durable process history (the historian question).** The continuously-recorded obligations — per-filter turbidity at least every 15 minutes retained ≥3 years [6, 9], the daily disinfection series [4], original continuous-monitoring recordings [1(j)(2)], and the interval energy data [10] — outlive the volatile ring by orders of magnitude. Decision 36 deliberately kept point history volatile and made only the journal durable; serving these records needs either a durable history sink in the same style (a monitor-local append file), a declared export seam to an external historian [15, 18], or a recorded decision that history persistence is deployment infrastructure. Which is a contract decision, not a ticket default.
2. **Period-boundary aggregation.** Daily lowest residuals, counts-and-percentages within limits, monthly summaries, running annual averages [4, 8], today's/yesterday's run hours [16, 17], and backwash statistics [7] are all calendar-period computations. No kind computes over a declared period, and the record is stamped in the tick domain — period boundaries need a declared tick→civil-time mapping before "per day" or "per month" is expressible at all. Whether period aggregation is in-contract (a period-accumulator or derived-value kind), computed by the export/reporting layer, or split (contract accumulates, downstream reduces) is a recorded decision.
3. **Computed derived values.** kW per 100 scfm, kWh per MG, kWh per unit load [13, 14] need arithmetic over two measured quantities — there is no derived-value kind (aeration note gap 8 records the same seam). Ratios are expressible only as external computation today.
4. **Durable equipment-fault and alarm event history.** Alarm activations and returns are Out-point transitions in the volatile ring; the journal carries quality changes, receipts, step failures, and role events — not alarm lifecycle transitions. A durable per-equipment fault/event history [19, 22] needs either journaled alarm transitions (interacting with the `WW-ALM-*` contract work) or a dedicated event record. Overlaps deployment-commissioning gap 6 (retention/export/integrity of the journal itself).
5. **Report artifacts and certified submission.** Forms, e-reports, schedules, and signatures [3, 5, 15, 21] are downstream of the control contract. If the platform generates report artifacts at all, the natural shape is an offline tool consuming journal and history exports — not a controller feature — but whether the product generates artifacts or stops at export is a product-boundary decision for the client to close.
6. **Export mechanism.** No bulk export of history or journal exists beyond the served `GET` endpoints and the journal file's line-delimited records; a historian feed, CMMS export, or reporting-layer handoff [15, 21, 23] needs an export format/transport decision (file artifacts, a documented query contract, or a connector).

## Proposed DCS implications

*All items in this section are proposals, not decided behavior.*

- Keep the controller's obligation at *trustworthy, timestamped, quality-stamped feedstock*: measured points, totals, counters, and the event record. The regulator-facing artifact (MOR, DMR, state form, certified e-submission) stays a reporting-layer product the platform feeds through export — matching where every cited owner and tool places it [3, 5, 15, 21].
- Add a durable history option in the journal file's pattern: a monitor-local append-only per-point record (or a decimated variant — e.g., declared recording intervals per point) so the 15-minute IFE record and multi-year retention expectations have a platform answer where no external historian exists [6, 9, 1(j)(2)]. The per-point recording declaration could be model metadata — the plant model naming which points carry regulatory recording duty and at what cadence — so recording scope is engineering data, not tooling configuration.
- Declare the tick→civil-time anchor once: a run's tick zero carries a wall-clock anchor the monitor stamps into history/journal export, so downstream period aggregation and report dates are computable without changing the deterministic core's timebase.
- If in-contract energy metrics are wanted, a small arithmetic derived-value kind (ratio of two Float inputs, quality-propagating) plus a period-accumulator (resettable totals held as last-period/current-period pairs — the "today's/yesterday's run hours" shape [17]) would cover kW/scfm, kWh/MG, and per-period counters without bespoke kinds; the alternative is feeding `totalizer`/`counter` outputs to the reporting layer and recording that choice.
- Feed maintenance exports from what already accumulates: expose `pump-group` run-hours and a `counter`-counted starts-per-pump as ordinary points a CMMS-facing export can map [16, 17, 23]; per-equipment fault history follows the gap-4 decision on durable alarm/event transitions.
- Let the alarm system's own monitoring metrics (flood, stale, frequency — `WW-ALM-004`) compute over the durable record it already requires, rather than adding a parallel reporting mechanism.

## Assumptions needing customer validation

- Which artifacts the client's regulators and operations staff actually require: the state MOR/DMR set and its cadence, internal daily/weekly operating reports, regulator audit extracts, and whether any artifact must be platform-generated versus produced in the client's existing historian/WIMS/LIMS layer [3, 5, 15, 21].
- Whether the deployment already includes a plant historian or data-management product the platform should feed [15, 18, 21] — if yes, the durable-history gap reduces to an export seam; if no, gap 1 becomes contract work.
- The event/operator record's retention and integrity expectations: how long the journal-class record must live, whether it must be exportable or tamper-evident, and whether alarm transitions must join the durable record [19; deployment-commissioning gap 6; security-identity note].
- Which period aggregates must be computed inside the contract versus downstream: today's/yesterday's run-hours [16, 17], daily minimum/maximum, monthly summaries — and the client's day boundary (calendar day, shift day, reporting month).
- Which derived metrics the client wants on the operator surface (kW per airflow, kWh/MG, wire-to-water efficiency [13, 14]) versus left to reporting — and whether any drive control (e.g., an efficiency alarm), which would pull the derived-value kind into contract.
- The maintenance-export form: tag-mapped points consumed by the client's CMMS [23], a periodic file export, or nothing beyond the journaled event record.
- Civil-time expectations on records: report dates, shift/day boundaries, and how a restart- or promotion-spanning run maps to them — the tick-domain record needs a declared anchoring convention before "per calendar day" records are verifiable.
- Whether certified electronic submission (NetDMR/state eDMR shapes [3]) is ever in scope, or permanently outside the product boundary.

**Revisit condition:** `WW-REP-001` stays a candidate. Promote when a first-client specification names a reporting obligation the platform itself must carry — a periodic report artifact or export format, a historian-feed or CMMS-export contract, a durable recording duty for named points (e.g., the per-filter 15-minute turbidity record), a journal retention/integrity requirement, or in-contract derived metrics — or when implementing `WW-CTL-004`/`WW-CTL-005` needs durable process history or computed derived values for their own acceptance evidence (the aeration note's gap 8 already reserves this seam). Until then the implemented surfaces — `totalizer`/`counter` accumulation, checkpointed run-hours, the volatile history ring, and the durable attributed journal — already feed every evidenced reporting behavior at the observable-telemetry level.
