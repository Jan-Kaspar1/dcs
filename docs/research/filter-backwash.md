# Filter backwash sequencing and shared-resource coordination

- **Checked:** 2026-09-15
- **Question:** What do owners, standards, and regulators actually declare for sequencing a bank of granular-media filters through backwash — the step sequence and its advance conditions, trigger sources and their precedence, arbitration for the shared air/water backwash supply, mid-sequence failure recovery, operator intervention, and the alarm set — so that `WW-CTL-004` can carry observable acceptance criteria?
- **Affected requirements:** `WW-CTL-004`

## Supported findings

### The backwash step sequence owners declare

- The canonical gravity-filter backwash is an ordered phase list, but the *content* varies by owner and media: surface-wash plants run surface wash → high-rate wash → low-rate restratification [13]; air-scour plants run drain-down → air scour → (optional combined air + low-rate water) → high-rate water wash → restratification/sub-fluidization → refill/repressurize → filter-to-waste [9, 15, 17, 20, 23]; twin-bed filters interleave the same phases per half-bed [9].
- Step advance is *both* time and measured state, by step: the cleaning phases are timed (setpoint/accumulator timer pairs per rate phase [10]; t=0/5/10/20 minute steps [12]; 4:00/6:40/2:00 surface/high/low steps [13]; 5 min scour + 5 min wash per side [9]), while the boundary steps commonly end on a measured condition — drain-down ends when the water level reaches the media surface [20], filter-to-waste runs until a turbidity bound *and/or* a volume bound is met (<0.2 NTU [12]; one full bed volume and <0.30 NTU [4]; ripening inside a 30-minute cap [15]), and a resting/settling period is an operator-adjustable dwell [15, 17].
- Rate structure inside the wash matters to owners: a low-rate transition step between air scour and high-rate wash lets entrained air escape [15]; the delivery rate must ramp rather than step to avoid media upset [1 §6.3.a.12 "prevent rapid changes in backwash water flow", 13, 17]; simultaneous air-plus-high-rate-water is a declared hazard some owners forbid outright, so the sequence itself must guard against it [15] — an interlock-like constraint inside the step pattern, not just a sequence-ordering choice.
- The phase vocabulary is standardized but the unit's valve/equipment pattern per phase is plant-specific: each phase maps to a declared combination of filter valves, backwash pump(s), and scour blower [10, 12, 23].

### Trigger conditions and precedence

- The convergent trigger set is elapsed filter run time, terminal headloss, effluent turbidity, and operator start — named as the three indicators (headloss, time, turbidity) plus manual start in regulator training and owner documents [3, 8, 9, 12, 17, 18] and in vendor defaults (schedule timer, ΔP threshold, HMI button [21]; pushbutton, timer, headloss, or turbidity breakthrough [20]).
- Whether automatic triggers may *start* a backwash is jurisdiction- and owner-specific, not universal: Utah *requires* the turbidity monitor to initiate automatic backwash above 0.3 NTU and mandates shutdown above 1 NTU [3]; Water Corporation auto-triggers on all three causes [8]; the Ten States-derived rules require operator-initiated backwash with operator-adjustable automation [1 §6.3.a.12]; Missouri's design guide forbids automatic initiation entirely [7]; Leopold's controls auto-initiate on filtrate quality and bed condition [19]. → precedence between automatic and operator initiation is a declared policy, and the *existence* of automatic initiation must itself be declarable.
- Where several triggers coexist, owners want to know which one fired: DS 114 requires a SCADA event recording which trigger type initiated each backwash [8]. Trigger identity is thus part of the sequence's observable record, not just an OR of conditions.
- A backwash *request* is distinguishable from a backwash *start*: the triggered filter is shut down and queued [8] or taken offline pending resource availability [9], so the trigger arms a request the arbiter later grants.

### Shared-resource arbitration

- One-at-a-time is the structural norm: the washwater system — backwash tank plus duplicate pumps, or the high-service main — serves one filter at a time at the declared rate [1 §6.3.a.12, 5, 6, 14]; rules size the supply for consecutive washes (≥2 filters back-to-back [3]) rather than concurrent ones; banks are explicitly sized to carry design flow with one filter out of service or in backwash [1 §6.3.a.3, 14]; pressure filters are filtered and backwashed individually [1 §6.3.b.3].
- Queue order is declared, not emergent: Te Marua washes first-in/first-out with an operator able to reorder the queue [9]; Water Corporation places each triggered filter in a backwash queue [8]. Which queue discipline the first client uses (FIFO, priority by trigger type — e.g., turbidity breakthrough ahead of routine time-based washes — or operator-managed) is a customer-validation question, but the *existence* of an ordered queue is owner-evidenced.
- A queued filter's meanwhile-state varies: DS 114 has the triggered filter shut down in the queue with a standby filter brought online to cover it [8]; Te Marua takes the filter offline until washwater reservoir level and recovery-plant capacity allow the wash [9]. Whether a queued filter keeps filtering until its grant or goes offline immediately — and which capacity permissives (reservoir level, waste-capacity headroom, online-flow disturbance <20% [8]) gate the grant — is declared per plant.
- The grant itself is conditional on shared-resource permissives: washwater supply available (tank level/pumps [9, 14]), downstream waste capacity [5's duplicate 120% waste pumps; 9's recovery-plant capacity], and the online-bank flow-disturbance bound [8].

### Failure recovery mid-sequence

- Mid-sequence equipment failure is a documented hazard: a washwater-valve actuator failure during a backwash nearly took down a full treatment plant [22].
- Owners specify abort, not silent resume: the Tulsa filter object carries an operator abort command and an aborted indication distinct from complete [10]; automated sequences must admit manual override [1, 7].
- The evidence thins at the fine semantics: the sources do not publish a uniform rule for which step a failed valve or blower returns the filter to, nor whether the sequence resumes in place after repair or aborts to a safe state and re-queues. The safe directions implied are: keep the bed covered and never apply air plus high-rate water together [15], hold the filter out of service until the operator or the sequence restores a clean path, and alarm the failure (equipment fault alarms plus the aborted status [10, 11]). The declared `on-fault` step and resume/abort choice is a contract parameter the customer must answer — see assumptions.

### Operator intervention

- The intervention vocabulary is consistent across owner systems: start a backwash manually [3, 7, 9, 12, 13, 21], abort a running one [10], move valves to/from automatic individually or as a bank [10], adjust the per-phase rates and durations as operator-tunable setpoints [7, 10, 15], reorder the queue [9], and run a graduated return-to-service ramp [1 §6.3.a.11, 6, 13].
- A per-step hold/advance/manual-step command surface is *not* evidenced in the owner sources — the sequencer's pause between phases and single-step jogging are common in integrator implementations [23] but the owner documents above express operator control as start/abort/override plus tunable step parameters, not free-form stepping. Whether hold/advance/manual-step is required operator surface is a customer-validation question.
- Operator authority is regulator-protected: manual override of automated backwash controls and operator-adjustable automation are explicit requirements [1 §6.3.a.12, 7].

### The alarm set

- Per-filter process alarms owners and rules declare: effluent turbidity above 0.3 NTU (mandated per-filter turbidimeter alarm [1 §6.3.a.11, 3, 6]), the 1 NTU shutdown tier [3], high/terminal headloss [8, 12, 18], and the trigger-source event record [8].
- Backwash-execution alarms: flow disturbance to online filters above the declared bound [8], clean-bed head loss outside its post-wash target band (an ineffective-wash detection [8]), ripening-period exceedances — per-event and monthly-aggregate [8, 15], backwash active/aborted/complete status [10].
- Equipment alarms compose from the shared per-class minimums — actuators, blowers, pumps, valves [11] — i.e., the failed-feedback/fault flags the `valve` and `motor` contracts already carry (a scour blower being a `motor` instance).
- Filter-to-waste and recycled-water obligations create record-keeping alarms rather than trip alarms: the alternate-ripening techniques require documented consistent use [4], and backwash water may not return to the head of the plant unsettled [3].

## Existing seams versus contract gaps

What the current component kinds, descriptors, and typed-build seam already express:

- Per-phase equipment: `valve` and `motor` carry command-with-feedback, `fault` on disagreeing feedback after `fault_ticks`, and the descriptor faceplate surface [10, 22]; `interlock`, `bool-gate`, and `sr-latch` express the phase-internal guards (no air + high-rate water together [15]; drain-down filtration-rate hold [8]).
- Timed phases: `sequencer` steps a declared table where each step drives `out` for `step_<n>_ticks` and reports `step`/`done`, with `run` hold and `reset` to step 1 — covering the timed phase spine [9, 10, 12, 13] — and its `capture_state`/`restore_state` already carries an in-flight sequence across checkpoint and promotion, which a 60–75-minute backwash needs [12].
- Trigger primaries: `timer`/`counter` express elapsed run time and filtered-volume accounting; `analog-input` plus `alarm-monitor`/`threshold-chain` express headloss and effluent-turbidity thresholds; a writable internal `In` point carries the operator-start request [3, 8, 9, 20, 21].
- Demand shaping: `rate-limiter` covers ramped backwash rate and graduated return-to-service [1, 13, 17]; `manual-station`/`override-select` cover operator takeover of a rate demand; `totalizer` covers backwash-volume accounting [10].
- Operator surface: writable internal `In` points plus `set_parameter` carry start/abort commands and per-phase rate/duration tuning [7, 10, 15]; `latching-alarm`/`bool-latching-alarm` compose the alarm set [3, 6, 8, 10, 11].
- Group precedent: `pump-group` demonstrates the indexed `x_i` per-member port convention and checkpointed group state — the pattern a bank coordinator would follow, though its semantics (stage all demanded members) are the opposite of mutual exclusion.

What they cannot express — the gap list WW-CTL-004 implementation tickets would consume:

1. **Measured-state step advance.** `sequencer` advances on `ticks` only; drain-down-until-level, waste-until-turbidity, and bed-volume bounds [4, 12, 20] need a per-step advance condition (ticks AND/OR a declared measured bound with a declared timeout/overrun behavior). → extend the sequencer contract or a dedicated backwash-sequence kind.
2. **Per-step equipment pattern.** `sequencer.out` is one Float; a backwash phase drives several valves plus pump and blower [10, 12, 23]. Options: a step-code `out` decoded by comparators/`bool-gate`s (wiring convention, no semantics), an Int step code consumed by per-valve `override-select` wiring, or declared per-phase outputs on a new kind. Which shape is a design decision for the implementing ticket.
3. **Shared-resource arbitration — the central question.** Nothing grants exclusive use of the backwash supply to one of N requesters, orders the queue, exposes queue position, or honors capacity permissives (reservoir level, waste capacity, flow-disturbance bound) [3, 5, 8, 9, 14]. `pump-group` stages multiple members on demand rather than serializing them; a FIFO-plus-reorder queue has no expression in `bool-gate`/`sr-latch` wiring; a `dcs-build` composition could emit the arbitration policy but with no runtime semantics or descriptor surface. → likely a new bank-coordinator kind (`request_i`/`grant_i`/`active`/`position_i` ports, declared queue policy and grant permissives); the alternatives — a documented wiring convention or builder-level composition — are the recorded candidates the decision must choose between.
4. **Trigger-source attribution.** DS 114's per-backwash record of which trigger fired [8] needs the OR of trigger inputs to also emit the firing source as a journaled/status value — a small declared output on the sequence/request path, not derivable after the fact from a plain `bool-gate` OR.
5. **Operator step surface.** Abort exists in owner evidence [10] but `sequencer` has only `run`/`reset` — no abort-to-declared-step, no hold/advance/manual-step. Which of these the contract needs is partly an open customer question, but abort-to-a-declared-safe-step is evidenced.
6. **Declared mid-sequence failure policy.** `sequencer` has no fault input and no `on_fault` step; a failed valve or blower must drive the filter to a declared step (hold, drain to safe, abort-and-requeue) [10, 15, 22] — a declared parameter on the sequence kind, since sources show owners require override and safe containment but leave resume-vs-abort to the plant's control narrative.
7. **Grant-gated sequence start.** The sequence must start only when the arbiter grants the shared supply — a permissive input on the sequence kind — and release the grant on completion or abort; plus the queued filter's meanwhile state (keep filtering vs. offline with standby cover [8, 9]) as a declared parameter.
8. **Post-wash verification and ripening monitoring.** CBHL-within-band [8] and ripening-NTU-within-duration [4, 8, 15] are phase-conditioned comparisons — "value measured during step X against a declared bound" — that `alarm-monitor` cannot express without a phase-gate; composable from an `interlock`-gated `alarm-monitor` fed by the active step, but the phase-capture idiom deserves a declared pattern.
9. **Bank-level events.** "Filter N backwash started/aborted/completed by trigger T" are journal-grade events with actor attribution — composable from `bool-latching-alarm` plus the journal once the coordinator exposes the status outputs; no new alarm contract needed.
10. **Filter-bank composition.** An N-filter bank with one coordinator, per-filter sequences, and the shared-supply equipment is a `dcs-build` assembly concern; whether the bank ships as a builder-level assembly spec, a `filter-bank` kind, or both is the planner's architecture decision (same shape as the station-assembly question in the pumping-station note).

## Proposed DCS implications

*All items in this section are proposals, not decided behavior.*

- Model each filter's backwash as a declared phase sequence: ordered steps each carrying a per-step equipment pattern, a duration bound, and a declared advance rule — timed, measured-state (level reached, turbidity below bound, bed volume delivered), or whichever-comes-first/last — matching the timed-plus-measured shape every owner document exhibits [4, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15, 20].
- Model the filter bank's backwash supply as a shared resource behind a declared arbiter: per-filter `request_i`/`grant_i` ports, a declared queue policy (FIFO as the evidenced default [8, 9]; operator reorder via the receipted command path), grant permissives (washwater supply available, waste capacity, online-flow disturbance bound [5, 8, 9, 14]), and status outputs (`active`, `position_i`, `resource_blocked`) that compose into the bank alarm set.
- Declare the queued filter's meanwhile state as a parameter — continue filtering until granted vs. take offline and call a standby filter — because owner documents evidence both [8, 9].
- Give the sequence kind a declared `on_fault` step and an abort command that drives the filter to a declared safe step rather than continuing or silently resetting; emit `aborted` distinct from `done` per the Tulsa vocabulary [10], and never let a step pattern violate phase-internal guards such as air-plus-high-rate-water exclusion [15].
- Make automatic triggers individually declarable per plant — elapsed run time, headloss, effluent turbidity, operator start — with the firing source recorded per backwash [8], and the auto-start permission itself a declared parameter because some regulators require operator initiation [1, 7] while others require automatic initiation [3].
- Carry sequence and queue state through checkpoints so a mid-backwash controller promotion does not drop the grant or restart a phase — the backwash-facing reach of `WW-LCM-001` (a proposal; sources do not address controller redundancy).
- Compose the alarm set from `latching-alarm`/`bool-latching-alarm` instances fed by the coordinator and sequence status outputs: per-filter effluent turbidity tiers (0.3 NTU alarm, 1 NTU shutdown [3, 6]), terminal headloss, trigger-attributed start events, backwash aborted/failed, resource-blocked queue, CBHL and ripening excursions [8, 10, 15].

## Assumptions needing customer validation

- Whether automatic triggers may *start* a backwash for the first client, or only queue a request for operator start — the sources mandate both directions in different jurisdictions [1, 3, 7].
- The queue discipline: FIFO [8, 9], priority-by-trigger (turbidity breakthrough ahead of routine time washes — plausible but not evidenced), or fully operator-managed ordering; and whether queue reorder is an operator function the contract must expose.
- The queued filter's meanwhile state — keep filtering until grant vs. offline immediately with standby cover [8, 9] — and the exact grant permissives (reservoir level, waste capacity, online-flow bound) the client's hydraulics require.
- The declared mid-sequence failure policy: which step a failed valve, pump, or blower returns the filter to, whether the sequence resumes after repair or aborts and re-queues, and whether the aborted filter returns to service without a completed wash.
- The operator step surface: start/abort/override are evidenced [1, 7, 10]; hold, advance, and manual-step are integrator practice [23] but not owner-evidenced — confirm whether the first client requires them.
- The step vocabulary itself: which phases exist for the client's filters (surface-wash vs. air-scour plants, twin-bed halves [9], ETS step [4, 17], resting period [15]) and which step ends are measured-state vs. timed.
- The alarm set's required latching and escalation: which of the trigger/execution alarms latch until acknowledged, and whether the turbidity tiers are alarm-only or shutdown-driving [3].
- Whether the shared-resource arbiter lands as a new component kind, a documented wiring convention, or a `dcs-build` composition — the central contract question implementation tickets must resolve first.
